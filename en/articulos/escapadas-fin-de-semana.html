Weekend getaways

Incredible places accessible by train from Sydney and Melbourne.

One of the best ways to start living in Australia is to leave the city without turning every plan into a major production. From Sydney and Melbourne, there are train getaways that help you rest, understand the landscape and experience another scale of the country.

The key is choosing well according to time, weather and energy. Some trips work perfectly as a day trip; others are better if you stay one night.

From Sydney

Blue Mountains. It is the classic getaway for a reason: nature, lookouts, walks and towns like Katoomba or Leura. Visit NSW notes that the region starts about 60 km from Sydney and can be reached by public transport. Ideal if you want a change of air without driving.

Kiama. South of Sydney, the coast changes the rhythm. The train follows part of the journey with ocean views, and Kiama works well for walking, seeing the blowhole, eating something and returning the same day. Destination Kiama mentions regular train services from Sydney and a particularly scenic coastal trip.

Newcastle. A good option if you want beach, coffee, bars and a smaller city with movement. Check timetables and trackwork before leaving, especially on weekends.

From Melbourne

Ballarat. Visit Victoria recommends Ballarat as a train trip from Southern Cross, with a journey of around 90 minutes towards the Goldfields region. It is good for history, architecture, gardens and a calm full-day plan.

Bendigo. If you are looking for art, food and a regional city with a strong identity, Bendigo works very well. It has gold rush history, galleries and an easy centre to walk.

Geelong. A little over an hour from Southern Cross, Geelong is a practical option for coast, waterfront and a low-logistics day out.

Before you go

  • Check timetables the day before: weekends can include trackwork or replacement buses.
  • Leave early if you want to avoid coming back tired and rushed.
  • Bring water, a light jacket and battery. Weather in Australia can change quickly.
  • Do not plan only from Instagram. Check real distances between the station, walks and food options.

These trips are not just tourism. For many people who migrate, they expand the mental map: Australia is not only the CBD, university or the first job. It is also the places where the country starts to feel more your own.
