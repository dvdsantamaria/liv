From GTE to GS

What changed and how to prepare clear answers for your Student Visa.

For years, the "GTE letter" was treated as a key part of a Student Visa application. But for Student Visa applications lodged from 23 March 2024, Home Affairs replaced the Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirement with the Genuine Student (GS) requirement.

The change matters. This is no longer about writing a long letter full of generic statements. The application now asks for more specific answers, in English, with a 150-word limit per answer and evidence to support what you say.

What Home Affairs looks at

According to Home Affairs, the GS requirement is designed to confirm that studying in Australia is the main reason for the visa. It also recognises something important: a genuine student may develop skills Australia needs and, if eligible in the future, explore later pathways. That future intention does not automatically work against the applicant.

The questions focus on four areas: your current circumstances, why you chose the course and provider, how the course benefits your future, and any other relevant information. If you have held a Student Visa before or are applying from Australia on another visa, there may be extra questions.

How to answer better

  • Connect your story to the course. Explain why the program makes sense based on your experience, previous studies or professional plan.
  • Show real research. Include details about the course, duration, requirements, cost, city and provider. Avoid answers that could apply to any institution.
  • Organise your evidence. Previous studies, employment, income, family ties, migration history and financial documents carry more weight than polished wording without support.
  • Be clear about changes. If there are study gaps, career changes, provider changes or an application from Australia, explain them with context.

The most common mistake

The most common mistake is trying to sound perfect. A strong answer is not the most decorated one; it is the most coherent one. There needs to be a clear line between your current situation, the course chosen, the reason for studying in Australia and the concrete benefit for your future.

That is why the application should be treated as a full case, not as an isolated letter. The GS answers and the documents need to tell the same story.

Important note

This guide is general information and does not replace migration advice. If you have previous refusals, course changes, a complex history or doubts about evidence, it is better to review the strategy before applying.
